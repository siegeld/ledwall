# The HUB320 connector

26 pins, 2x13. Four RGB groups where HUB75 carries two, which is the whole
point of it: twelve data lines instead of six, so a high-density module shifts
less per scan step.

**HUB320 is not an interoperability specification.** It is a shape that several
manufacturers fill in differently. Pins 1-19 and 22-26 are consistent across
every source seen so far; **pins 20 and 21 are not**, and that is the trap this
page exists to record.

## The hazard: we drive pins 20 and 21

Our card drives all five of A-E. On a module built to column 3 that means:

* **pin 20** — row-address bit D is driven onto **S-CS**, the chip select of
  the module's serial configuration bus, and toggled at row-scan rate.
* **pin 21** — row-address bit E is driven onto **S-MI**, which is a serial
  data line **out of the module**. That is an FPGA output against a module
  output: contention, not merely a wrong logic level.

This is harmless on the bench module, whose silkscreen marks both pins NC —
which is why nothing has been damaged. It is **not** safe to assume of the next
module.

> **Before plugging any new module into an E320, read its connector silkscreen
> at pins 20 and 21.** If they are anything other than NC or D/E, do not use a
> bitstream that drives the full five-line row block.

With the current `dp3264s` table (`row_address=1`, `row_addr_shift=3`), the row
value is `scan_row >> 3`, which for 64 rows never exceeds 7 — so D and E are
held at 0 rather than toggling. With `row_addr_shift=0` (gateware 1.5.0, the
`row & 7` experiment) the full 0-63 reaches all five lines and D and E do
toggle. Neither is safe against an S-MI that is driving.

## What three address lines can and cannot do

Three binary lines select eight states. A 64-scan module therefore **cannot**
be row-addressed by A/B/C alone. The possibilities, none yet proven on our
module:

1. **A/B/C are the high or low three bits**, and the driver IC's internal
   counter supplies the rest. Both mappings have been tried on hardware
   (`row & 7` and `row >> 3`) and both left the panel dark. See CHANGELOG
   2.27.0.
2. **A/B/C are not an address at all** but serial control into the cascaded
   row drivers — eight `5958` chips of eight outputs being exactly 64. This is
   the standing hypothesis.
3. **A separate serial bus configures the row drivers**, via S-CK/S-MO/S-CS/
   S-MI, and A/B/C then address only the eight groups that bus has set up.
   Column 3 is the first evidence this bus exists; our module has no S-CS or
   S-MI, which argues against it being used there.

### Corroboration for (2), from the vendor's own software

Colorlight's `LEDSetting` was decoded on `sendagi` on 2026-10-17, read-only,
with a packet capture confirming nothing was transmitted:

* `src/utils/chip.ts` maps `decodeChipId 3` to `DM5953/5958` — our module's row
  chip, and the id both CD-P1.2-256X128 DP3264 profiles report.
* `decodeChip/componentsMap.ts` routes `DM5953/5958` to the **595 shift-register
  editor** (`chips/Normal/595`), the same editor used for a plain `595` — **not**
  the `138` binary decoder, which has its own entry.
* That editor's only field is `frequencyDivisionCoef`, a clock divider. There is
  no address-width, address-line-count or row-map field in it, nor in any of the
  other twenty-odd decode-chip editors.
* **No Colorlight profile encodes how A/B/C select a row.** The receiver firmware
  derives it from `decodeChipId` plus `scanNumber` plus the timing quartet
  (blanking, rowSwitching, startTime, endTime). Eleven profiles were decoded;
  raw output is on `sendagi` in `scratchpad/decode/rows.json`.

A clock divider on the row chip fits a serially clocked row driver. It does not
fit a binary decoder. That is inference from the source, not a comment in it.

## Still unknown

* Which of pins 17/18/19 is data, which is clock, and which is latch or enable,
  and the polarity of each. Four unknowns; asked of the module's supplier on
  2026-10-17.
* Whether B1 and B2 (pins 3 and 7) double as S-CK and S-MO on our module. If
  they do, we are currently driving blue pixel data onto a serial bus.
* Whether the `.rcvbp` receiver configuration file the supplier uses on a stock
  E320 would settle all of it at once. Also asked.
